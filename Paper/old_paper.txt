Jeffreys Flow: Robust Boltzmann Generators for Multimodal
Distributions via Distillation of Parallel Tempering

Abstract
Boltzmann Generators and Stochastic Normalizing Flows have emerged as powerful frameworks for sampling complex target distributions, but their application to multimodal landscapes
remains challenging due to mode collapse. We introduce Jeffreys Flow, a novel training framework that unifies the Energy KL (reverse KL) and Data KL (forward KL) divergences via the
Jeffreys divergence. This symmetric objective ensures both global mode coverage and local
sampling precision, enabling robust distillation of high-quality samples from Parallel Tempering
trajectories. We provide a rigorous error analysis of the single-step transport map, establish
its superiority over single-divergence training, and present a sequential distillation strategy that
extracts high-fidelity flows from Parallel Tempering. The framework seamlessly integrates with
Replica Exchange Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (reSGLD) for finite-sum potentials
and with Path Integral Monte Carlo (PIMC) for quantum thermal sampling. Extensive numerical experiments across Gaussian mixtures, Rosenbrock, Rastrigin, solvated periodic systems,
and quantum harmonic oscillators demonstrate superior sampling efficiency, robustness, and
accelerated convergence compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

Introduction

Boltzmann Generators [1] and Stochastic Normalizing Flows (SNF) [2] have emerged as powerful frameworks for sampling complex, high-dimensional target distributions π(x) ∝ e−U (x) that
arise in computational physics, statistical inference, and molecular dynamics. By constructing diffeomorphic transport maps from a simple source distribution to the target, these methods enable
efficient generation of configurations without the need for long Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC)
simulations. Nevertheless, the sampling of multimodal distributions—where the energy landscape
U (x) contains multiple metastable basins separated by high barriers—remains a fundamental challenge. In this regime, the gold-standard method is Parallel Tempering (PT) [3], which uses replica
exchange across a temperature ladder to ensure ergodic exploration.
Applying flow-based samplers to multimodal problems is notoriously difficult due to the pervasive issue of mode collapse. When a normalizing flow is trained by minimizing the Energy KL
(reverse KL) divergence, the objective is inherently mode-seeking: the flow tends to concentrate
probability mass on a single low-energy mode while ignoring other metastable states. Conversely,
training with the Data KL (forward KL) divergence—equivalent to maximum likelihood estimation
on a finite dataset—forces the flow to cover the entire support of the training samples, but its
accuracy is strictly bounded by the quality and completeness of those samples. If the training data
1

fail to cross certain energy barriers, the learned flow will never discover the missing modes, and
there is no mechanism to correct the bias using the physical energy function.
Recent efforts to overcome mode collapse have largely focused on modifying the training objective or introducing auxiliary processes. Techniques such as LREX [4] and TemperFlow [5] employ
temperature annealing to gradually bridge the gap between a simple prior and the complex target.
Conditional Boltzmann Generators [6] rely on predefined reaction coordinates to steer sampling
across barriers. While effective, these methods often incur substantial computational overhead due
to sequential training ladders, continuous auxiliary simulations, or the need for domain-specific
collective variables.
Our fundamental philosophy diverges from these approaches and is notably simple: Parallel
Tempering is already highly effective at exploring multimodal landscapes. Instead of trying to design
a flow-based sampler that outperforms PT in raw exploration capability, we aim to distill the
knowledge already captured by PT into a compact, fast-to-evaluate normalizing flow. By leveraging
the high-quality, ergodically mixed samples produced by PT, we can train flows that faithfully
reproduce the full multimodal geometry without requiring expensive annealing schedules or manual
tuning of reaction coordinates.
To achieve bidirectional fidelity—i.e., to ensure that the learned flow both covers all modes
present in the PT samples (Data-KL property) and concentrates probability mass correctly within
each mode (Energy-KL property)—we advocate for the Jeffreys divergence, the symmetrized KL
divergence, as the optimal training objective. The Jeffreys divergence is a convex combination of
the Energy KL and Data KL terms, providing a unified loss that simultaneously enforces global
mode coverage and local sampling precision. We refer to the resulting method as Jeffreys Flow.
The Jeffreys Flow framework offers several key advantages: it provides theoretical robustness,
as the symmetric objective possesses a unique minimizer that acts as a physics-informed regularizer,
guaranteeing a transport map whose KL divergence strictly outperforms the empirical target data
while suppressing spurious modes. It also offers practical flexibility, with a variance-based scaling
mechanism that automatically tunes the weighting between the two KL terms, requiring only a
single hyperparameter θ ∈ [0, 1]. Furthermore, the framework seamlessly integrates with established
samplers, naturally combining with Parallel Tempering for distillation, with Replica Exchange
Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (reSGLD) for large-scale finite-sum potentials, and with
Path Integral Monte Carlo (PIMC) for quantum thermal sampling.
The main contributions of this paper are: (i) Introduction of Jeffreys Flow, a unified normalizing flow training framework that bridges the Energy KL and Data KL divergences via the
Jeffreys divergence; (ii) rigorous error analysis of the single-step transport map obtained under
the Jeffreys objective, establishing its superiority over single-divergence training; (iii) a sequential
distillation strategy that extracts high-fidelity flows from Parallel Tempering trajectories, enabling
one-shot generation of complex multimodal distributions; (iv) extension to stochastic-gradient settings through integration with reSGLD, including auxiliary networks for importance-weight estimation and variance-reduced rejuvenation; (v) application to quantum thermal sampling via a
dimensionality-truncated flow trained on the low-frequency normal modes of a ring-polymer discretization, with systematic error correction through importance reweighting; and (vi) comprehensive numerical validation across a range of benchmark problems—Gaussian mixtures, Rosenbrock,
Rastrigin, solvated periodic systems, and quantum harmonic oscillators—demonstrating superior
sampling efficiency, robustness, and accelerated convergence compared to state-of-the-art baselines.

samplers for multimodal distributions. Section 3 introduces the Jeffreys divergence and derives the
single-step training objective. Section 4 details the sequential distillation procedure using Parallel
Tempering. Sections 5 and 6 present applications to reSGLD and Path Integral Monte Carlo,

Related Works

The development of normalizing flows for sampling complex target distributions has seen rapid
progress over the past decade. The foundational Boltzmann Generators [1] and Stochastic Normalizing Flows (SNF) [2] established the paradigm of training invertible neural networks by minimizing
the Energy KL (reverse KL) divergence, which directly utilizes the target potential energy U (x).
This approach yields high-fidelity samples in low-energy regions but is intrinsically mode-seeking
and suffers from severe mode collapse when the energy landscape contains multiple metastable
basins separated by high barriers.
To mitigate mode collapse, a first line of research replaces the Energy KL with the Data KL
(forward KL) objective. Gabrié et al. [7] train flows via Maximum Likelihood Estimation on samples
collected on-the-fly from an adaptive MCMC chain. While this ensures mass-covering behavior, the
resulting flow is strictly bounded by the quality of the underlying sampler; any bias or incomplete
exploration of the training data is inherited by the generative model without a physical correction
mechanism.
A second line of work employs temperature annealing to gradually bridge a simple prior to the
complex target. LREX (Learned Replica Exchange) [4] trains a flow to map configurations from a
fast-mixing high-temperature replica directly to the low-temperature target, thereby skipping the
intermediate temperature ladder of conventional parallel tempering. TemperFlow [5] replaces the
KL objective with the squared L2 Wasserstein distance, theoretically guaranteeing non-vanishing
gradients across isolated modes. These methods effectively cover multimodal supports but require
sequential training of multiple networks or continuous auxiliary simulations, incurring substantial
computational overhead.
A third category leverages predefined reaction coordinates to steer the flow across barriers.
Conditional Boltzmann Generators (CBG) [6] condition the flow on a reaction coordinate, enabling
explicit scanning of transition pathways. This approach is powerful when suitable collective variables are known, but it relies on domain-specific knowledge and does not generalize to problems
where such coordinates are unavailable.
More recent strategies dynamically adjust the annealing schedule based on sample-quality metrics. Wang et al. [8] propose an adaptive tempering scheme that enforces a minimum Effective
Sample Size (ESS) between consecutive intermediate distributions, ensuring sufficient overlap to
avoid mode collapse. While theoretically sound, the resulting temperature ladder can become
prohibitively fine-grained, and the optimization often stagnates at challenging phase transitions.
Flow-based samplers have also been adopted in industrial and engineering settings for rare-event
simulation and uncertainty quantification. Adaptive MCMC with normalizing-flow proposals [7] has
been applied to high-dimensional inverse problems in geophysics and medical imaging. Tempered
distribution flows [5] have been used for failure-probability estimation in structural engineering.
Conditional Boltzmann Generators have been employed for free-energy reconstruction in molecular
dynamics and drug discovery. These applications highlight the practical importance of robust
multimodal sampling, but they also expose the limitations of existing methods: either they rely on
costly sequential annealing, require manual tuning of reaction coordinates, or are limited by the
quality of the training data.
Unlike the above approaches, Jeffreys Flow does not attempt to replace or out-perform Parallel
Tempering (PT) in raw exploration capability. Instead, it adopts a distillation philosophy: PT is

3

already highly effective at exploring multimodal landscapes, and we can leverage its high-quality,
ergodically mixed samples to train a compact normalizing flow that faithfully reproduces the full
multimodal geometry. The key innovation is the use of the Jeffreys divergence—the symmetrized
KL divergence—as the training objective. By combining the Energy KL (which enforces local
precision) and the Data KL (which enforces global mode coverage) into a single convex loss, Jeffreys Flow achieves a robust balance that eliminates mode collapse while retaining the physical
constraints of the target distribution. This unified framework avoids sequential annealing (no need
for temperature ladders), requires no reaction coordinates (fully unconditional), is compatible with
stochastic-gradient settings via integration with reSGLD, and extends to quantum thermal sampling via dimensionality-truncated flows for Path Integral Monte Carlo.
Thus, Jeffreys Flow provides a versatile, one-shot generation tool that combines the strengths
of physics-based and data-driven training, offering a distinct alternative to existing flow-based
samplers for multimodal problems.

Single-Step Training with Jeffreys Divergence

In this section, we introduce the Jeffreys divergence and demonstrate its superiority over singledirection Energy KL or Data KL in generating high-quality samples through a theoretical argument.
We begin by considering the single-step transport problem. Let U0 (x) and U1 (x) be two known
potential functions on Rd , defining the distributions π0 (x) ∝ e−U0 (x) and π1 (x) ∝ e−U1 (x) , respectively. Since π0 and π1 are not directly tractable, we assume access to two distributions µ0 and µ1 ,
which serve as empirical approximations consisting of samples from π0 and π1 . Our objective is to
design a normalizing flow F on Rd such that the pushforward distribution satisfies F# π0 = π1 .

3.2

Advantages of Jeffreys Divergence

While the Energy KL divergence KL(F# π0 ∥π1 ) is theoretically capable of generating high-fidelity
samples, it is intrinsically prone to mode collapse, as highlighted in [5]. This limitation arises
because the Energy KL is mode-seeking; specifically, if the generated distribution F# π0 captures
only a subset of the local modes of π1 while ignoring others, the divergence value can still remain
negligible. Consequently, the Energy KL objective fails to distinguish between the true target
distribution and a collapsed approximation that omits significant regions of the probability mass.
Incorporating the Data KL objective effectively mitigates mode collapse, as the divergence
KL(π1 ∥F# π0 ) diverges to infinity if the generated distribution F# π0 fails to cover any mode where
π1 has significant mass. However, if the flow is trained exclusively using the Data KL, the quality
of the generated samples is inherently bounded by the fidelity of the empirical approximation µ1
to the true target π1 .

We scale the loss weights by the variance to implicitly balance the gradients: when transforming

driven by the Energy KL.
The algorithm’s performance is generally robust to the specific choice of θ. However, excessively
large values (biasing towards Energy KL) can still trigger sudden mode collapse, resulting in a
precipitous drop in CESS. In practice, we determine the strategy for θ by monitoring CESS and
other performance parameters. Theoretically, the framework supports adaptive strategies—such
as dynamically adjusting θ or introducing additional PT sampling temperatures—but we do not
implement such adaptive mechanisms in this article.

We remark that evaluating the exact energy difference required for the standard MetropolisHastings acceptance step in MALA is computationally prohibitive due to the finite-sum structure of
the target potential function. To address this, we replace the standard MALA with an Unadjusted
Langevin Algorithm (ULA) governed by a Stochastic Variance-Reduced Gradient (SVRG) estimator. Specifically, at the beginning of the rejuvenation phase, we compute the exact full gradient at
an initial reference point. During the subsequent Langevin iterations, we compute the stochastic
gradients using random mini-batches and adjust them using the pre-computed full gradient to significantly reduce the variance. This variance-reduced stochastic gradient minimizes the temporal
discretization error in the simulated Langevin dynamics. Consequently, we can safely bypass the
Metropolis accept-reject step—effectively accepting every proposal—while still maintaining high
asymptotic accuracy for the generated ensemble.
Furthermore, we discuss the approximation errors inherent in the combined reSGLD and Jeffreys
Flow framework. Similar to the standard formulation, the accuracy of the generated distribution
hinges strictly on the unbiasedness of the importance sampling weights. If these weights are neglected or inaccurately approximated by the auxiliary neural networks, approximation errors will
unroll and amplify exponentially along the temperature ladder. This sensitivity imposes a stringent precision requirement on the weight function training. Fortunately, the MSE loss function
governing ϕ and ψ is generally well-behaved and straightforward to minimize, preventing weight
estimation from becoming a computational bottleneck. Ultimately, with adequately optimized

weights, the framework not only achieves sampling precision comparable to the baseline reSGLD,
but significantly surpasses it in generating independent samples at a vastly accelerated rate.
Despite the fact that the unbiased property of stochastic gradients allows for the correct training of auxiliary networks ϕ and ψ to approximate the weight function, in many practical scenarios,
it is still highly recommended to use the native, exact formulation to compute the precise weight
function. This recommendation is supported by two primary reasons: First, neural network approximations inherently introduce natural errors, whereas exact sampling imposes an exceptionally high
demand on the precision of the weight function. In contrast, the training of the normalizing flow
itself is far less sensitive to such precision requirements, meaning that utilizing stochastic gradients
for flow optimization typically incurs no noticeable degradation in accuracy. Second, computing
the exact weight function requires evaluating the full potential Uk only once at the conclusion of
each temperature step, which generally does not impose a significant computational burden. By
comparison, the frequent temperature exchanges inherent to reSGLD demand evaluating energy
differences repeatedly, resulting in a substantially higher asymptotic complexity. Therefore, the
computational cost of a single exact evaluation for the weight function is entirely justifiable and
acceptable.

Conclusion

This paper introduced Jeffreys Flow, a unified normalizing-flow training framework that bridges the
Energy KL (reverse KL) and Data KL (forward KL) divergences via the Jeffreys divergence. The
main contributions are: (i) theoretical foundation (derived the Jeffreys divergence loss as a convex
combination of Energy KL and Data KL, proved its strict convexity, and established that the resulting transport map strictly outperforms the empirical target distribution (Theorem 1), with stability
guarantees for importance weights (Lemma 1, Theorem 2)); (ii) sequential distillation strategy (developed a practical sequential distillation procedure that extracts high-fidelity flows from Parallel
Tempering trajectories, maintaining high Conditional Effective Sample Size across temperature ladders); (iii) extension to stochastic-gradient settings (integrated Jeffreys Flow with Replica Exchange
Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics, overcoming inherent bias of stochastic swap acceptances
via auxiliary neural networks and variance-reduced rejuvenation); (iv) application to quantum thermal sampling (adapted Jeffreys Flow to Path Integral Monte Carlo via a dimensionality-truncated
Fourier-mode representation, enabling scalable sampling of ring-polymer configurations while preserving quantum effects); and (v) comprehensive numerical validation (demonstrated effectiveness
31

across diverse benchmark problems, showing superior sampling efficiency, robustness, and accelerated convergence).
Jeffreys Flow offers several key advantages over existing flow-based samplers: bidirectional fidelity (by symmetrizing the KL divergence, it simultaneously enforces global mode coverage via
Data KL and local sampling precision via Energy KL, effectively eliminating mode collapse); distillation philosophy (rather than attempting to outperform Parallel Tempering in raw exploration,
Jeffreys Flow distills the knowledge already captured by PT into a compact, fast-to-evaluate generator); flexibility (the framework seamlessly integrates with established sampling algorithms (PT,
reSGLD, PIMC) and can be applied to bounded periodic domains, high-dimensional finite-sum
potentials, and infinite-dimensional path integrals); and scalability (through mode truncation and
importance reweighting, Jeffreys Flow scales to high-dimensional and quantum problems without
prohibitive computational overhead).
While the present work establishes a solid theoretical and empirical foundation, several promising avenues remain for future research: adaptive weighting (develop online strategies for tuning the
balancing parameter θ based on real-time metrics such as CESS or gradient variances); theoretical
extensions (analyze the convergence rate of the sequential distillation process and derive explicit
error bounds for the truncated Fourier-mode approximation in PIMC); broader applications (apply
Jeffreys Flow to challenging real-world problems such as molecular dynamics with explicit solvents,
high-dimensional Bayesian inverse problems, and lattice field theories); integration with diffusion
models (explore connections between Jeffreys Flow and score-based generative models, potentially
unifying the benefits of both paradigms); and hardware-aware implementation (optimize the framework for GPU-based distributed training and inference, enabling real-time sampling for extremely
large-scale simulations).
Jeffreys Flow provides a versatile, physics-informed framework for accelerating rare-event sampling in computational physics, statistics, and machine learning. By unifying energy-based and
data-driven training objectives, it delivers robust, high-fidelity generators that overcome the fundamental limitations of single-divergence methods. The experimental results confirm that Jeffreys
Flow not only matches but often surpasses the sampling quality of gold-standard MCMC techniques while offering orders-of-magnitude speed-ups through parallel, feed-forward generation. We
believe this work opens a new pathway toward efficient, reliable generative modeling for complex
multimodal distributions.
